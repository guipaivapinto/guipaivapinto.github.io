Wakanda: scarcity, opportunity cost, and trade

Models · Fictional classroom economy

Wakanda let us isolate an economic problem: limited resources can produce different combinations of goods. The production possibilities frontier describes those choices. Moving along it changes the allocation of existing resources; moving the frontier changes productive capacity. The numbers describe an invented economy, so we can focus on the model.

Read the graph

Read the two goods on the axes. Points on the frontier are feasible and productively efficient; points inside leave capacity unused. Opportunity cost is the output forgone when one good increases.

Connect to the concept: Comparative advantage depends on opportunity cost, not simply who can produce more.

Optional quick practice

An economy moves along an unchanged PPF to produce more of one good. What happens to the other good on a downward-sloping frontier?

  1. Its output falls.
  2. Its output must also rise.
  3. Its output stays fixed.
  4. Its price must fall.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Its output falls.

Moving along a downward-sloping frontier trades one output for another. It does not by itself change prices or expand capacity.

The Miracle of Bern

Models · 1954

The 1954 World Cup final in Bern gives us an opening question about predictions. An outcome can surprise observers without proving that every useful model is worthless. Economic models simplify a situation, making assumptions that help us explain a mechanism and understand why outcomes can differ from expectations.

Interpret the example

The historical images establish the setting. They are not economic data or a test of a statistical model. The event took place in Switzerland; West Germany and Hungary were the teams.

Historical context
  1. 1954 — West Germany and Hungary meet in the final in Bern, Switzerland.

Connect to the concept: Evaluate a model by its assumptions, purpose, and evidence.

Optional quick practice

What is the strongest conclusion from a surprising outcome?

  1. Every model is useless.
  2. A prediction needs to be evaluated with its assumptions and uncertainty.
  3. The winning outcome was certain in advance.
  4. One event establishes a universal economic law.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. A prediction needs to be evaluated with its assumptions and uncertainty.

A surprising result invites examination of the prediction and its uncertainty; it does not establish a universal verdict on modeling.

South Korea: crisis, the gold campaign, and recovery

Growth · 1997–98; long-run comparison 1960–2024

South Korea’s financial crisis interrupted a much longer expansion in output per person. The gold-collection campaign is a memorable part of the response, but the wider explanation includes foreign-currency debt, financing difficulties, restructuring, and recovery. This case connects short-run fluctuations with the productive capacity that supports growth over decades.

Read the graph

Follow real GDP per person over time. A recession is a decline over part of the path; sustained growth concerns the longer trajectory. The campaign photograph illustrates a response, not the size of its causal contribution.

Historical context
  1. 1997 — Foreign-currency financing pressures deepen the Korean crisis.
  2. 1998 — The gold campaign accompanies a broader crisis response.
  3. Long run — GDP per person grows across decades despite downturns.

Connect to the concept: Recovery toward previous output and long-run growth are different processes.

Optional quick practice

Output falls sharply and then returns to its previous level. What does that return show most directly?

  1. A permanent doubling of productive capacity.
  2. The end of all future recessions.
  3. Recovery from the contraction.
  4. A change in the definition of GDP.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. Recovery from the contraction.

Returning to a prior output level is recovery. It alone does not establish a higher long-run productivity trend.

BTS, HYBE, and the wider economy

Output & prices · 2022 market event; separate government impact estimate

The BTS/HYBE example separates company news, financial-market prices, and production. A share price reflects investors’ views about future returns. GDP measures current production. Cultural exports may affect several industries, but an estimated spillover is a model-based calculation, not the same thing as an observed fall in a stock price.

Read the graph

Read the HYBE price and trading-volume series using their own axes. The government economic-impact estimate is separate evidence with separate assumptions. A dollar lost in market valuation is not automatically a dollar lost in GDP.

Historical context
  1. June 2022 — News about BTS is followed by a sharp HYBE market-price response.

Connect to the concept: Asset prices and the value of newly produced goods and services are different quantities.

Optional quick practice

HYBE’s share price falls after news about BTS. Which statement follows directly?

  1. Korean GDP falls by exactly the same percentage.
  2. Every Korean export falls.
  3. The number of Korean jobs falls by the same amount.
  4. Investors now trade the shares at a lower price.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. Investors now trade the shares at a lower price.

A share-price movement directly establishes a change in the market price of that asset. Additional evidence is needed for effects on production or jobs.

U.S. downturns and stabilization policy

Output & prices · Great Depression; postwar policy; Volcker disinflation

The Great Depression and the Volcker disinflation illustrate different macroeconomic policy problems. During a deep contraction, weak spending and financial disruption can leave resources idle. Reducing entrenched inflation can also involve a period of weak output and high unemployment. The policy objective and the economic conditions matter for the mechanism.

Interpret the example

Read the dated accounts as historical explanations. They do not isolate a controlled effect of one person or policy. National evidence belongs at the U.S. level, not at the location of the Federal Reserve’s offices.

Historical context
  1. 1929–33 — The Depression brings a severe contraction and banking distress.
  2. 1934–48 — Marriner Eccles leads the Federal Reserve.
  3. 1979–87 — Paul Volcker leads the Federal Reserve during disinflation.

Connect to the concept: Stabilization addresses fluctuations around productive capacity.

Optional quick practice

Why is the Volcker episode relevant to stabilization policy?

  1. Reducing inflation can involve short-run output and employment costs.
  2. A fall in inflation necessarily means all prices fall.
  3. Interest rates determine GDP by definition.
  4. Disinflation eliminates structural unemployment.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. Reducing inflation can involve short-run output and employment costs.

Disinflation can involve weaker demand and employment. It means slower price growth, not necessarily a falling price level.

Output, debt, and life expectancy across countries

Output & prices · 1970–2024 GDP; other series have separate windows

The macroeconomic overview compared production, public debt, and life expectancy. Each indicator answers a different question. A country can produce more per person without having the same debt burden or health outcomes as another country. We use these comparisons to see why one national statistic cannot summarize every dimension of living standards.

Read the graph

Switch between the three class figures. Keep each unit and time window visible. An index of GDP, a debt-to-GDP ratio, and years of life expectancy cannot be put on one meaningful common numerical scale.

Connect to the concept: Choose an indicator to match the question.

Optional quick practice

Which indicator directly measures a health outcome rather than production?

  1. Another country’s GDP per person in the same price base.

  3. GDP per person for the next year.
  4. A GDP-per-person growth rate calculated from the same series.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: B. Life expectancy, measured in years.

Life expectancy measures years of life. It is a distinct outcome, even when it is considered alongside income.

Denmark: pharmaceuticals and measured GDP

Output & prices · 2019–2025 saved GDP series; pharmaceutical expansion

Pharmaceutical expansion made an important contribution to Denmark’s measured economic growth. The example links an industry’s activity to national value added. It also raises an accounting question: production arrangements and economic ownership can be more complicated than locating a company’s headquarters or a factory on a map.

Read the graph

Compare the GDP path and pharmaceutical contribution in the class figure. Keep the saved data vintage and units. A sector’s contribution to growth is different from its total share of GDP or the market value of a company.

Historical context
  1. 2019–2025 — The saved class series follows Denmark’s economy and pharmaceutical expansion.
  2. November 2023 — Novo Nordisk announces a major Kalundborg capacity expansion.

Connect to the concept: GDP records production and value added; it is not a company valuation.

Optional quick practice

A pharmaceutical company’s share price doubles. What can we conclude about Denmark’s GDP from that fact alone?

  1. GDP doubles.
  2. Pharmaceutical production doubles.
  3. The GDP effect cannot be determined from the share price alone.
  4. Every Danish household’s income doubles.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: C. The GDP effect cannot be determined from the share price alone.

The stock market values claims on future returns. GDP requires evidence about current production and value added.

Medicine prices: U.S. and international comparisons

Output & prices · Reported prices in September 2024

The medicine-price comparison asked what a quoted price actually measures. A reported list price is not necessarily a patient’s payment, a pharmacy’s transaction price, or a manufacturer’s receipt after rebates. That distinction matters when connecting a news comparison to the consumer price index and to changes in treatment quality.

Read the graph

The figure reports selected monthly Ozempic prices from September 2024. Read the source definition before comparing countries. The class discussion concerns price measurement, not which treatment a person should choose.

Connect to the concept: Different price concepts can produce different comparisons.

Optional quick practice

Which statement best interprets a reported medicine list price?

  1. It is every patient’s copayment.
  2. It equals the manufacturer’s receipt after every rebate.
  3. It is a direct measure of treatment effectiveness.
  4. It is a quoted price that may differ from transaction prices and patient payments.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: D. It is a quoted price that may differ from transaction prices and patient payments.

The list price is a specific price concept. Insurance arrangements, rebates, and transactions can produce different amounts.

U.S. GDP and consumer-price data

Output & prices · Use dates and base years in each saved series

GDP and consumer-price data help separate quantities from prices. Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP adjusts for price changes. CPI follows consumer purchases, while the GDP deflator covers domestically produced final output. The appropriate measure depends on whether we are studying production, consumer purchasing power, or a particular basket.

Interpret the example

An index level is not an inflation rate. Inflation is a percentage change in an index between specified dates. Preserve the index base, frequency, and adjustment status when downloading or comparing series.

Connect to the concept: Match the price index to the economic quantity.

Optional quick practice

A price index rises from 100 to 105. What is inflation over that interval?

  1. 5%.
  2. 105%.
  3. 0.05%.
  4. An increase of 5 percentage points in the unemployment rate.
Show answer and explanation

Answer: A. 5%.

Inflation is (105 − 100) / 100 × 100 = 5%. The new index level is 105, not 105% inflation.
