The explanations, transaction examples, accounting-table design, numerical exercises and model diagrams were independently written and drawn for this course.

Euro–dollar exchange rate

Federal Reserve Board, H.10 historical daily exchange rates: U.S. dollars per euro, January 3–December 30, 2022. The original line chart uses 250 reported observations, omits dates marked ND, and does not interpolate missing dates. The gold line marks parity at one dollar per euro. Data retrieved November 23, 2026. The subsequent teaching tables quote foreign currency per U.S. dollar; the introductory slide states the reciprocal relationship.

The Board website policy places its information in the public domain unless otherwise indicated. Only official numerical observations are used; no Board logo, source chart or separately credited third-party item is reproduced.

U.S. balance of payments

U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, U.S. International Transactions, Fourth Quarter and Year 2022, March 23, 2023 release, Table 1, physical PDF pages 9–10. These are the historical March 2023 estimates, not later revisions. Original amounts in millions of current dollars are divided by 1,000 and rounded independently to whole billions. Factor income corresponds to primary income; transfers correspond to secondary income.

The financial account is shown as net capital inflow, reversing BEA’s net-lending/net-borrowing sign. The original table shows asset transactions and net financial derivatives separately; the resulting financial-account balance is +677 billion dollars. The final +267 billion-dollar row explicitly combines the capital-account balance and the official statistical discrepancy. It is not the statistical discrepancy alone. The simplified identity CA = −FA sets aside those additional entries.

BEA’s public-domain information policy supports use of these official numerical observations. The table is independently designed; no publisher screenshot or source-table artwork is reproduced.

Models and instructional examples

The two-country loanable-funds diagrams and dollar-market curves are original schematic drawings from the stated economic relationships. Currency amounts, Period 1/2 quotations, linear demand/supply schedules and the inflation/basket-price examples are instructional values, not current market data. Independent curve shifts retain the same baseline. The exchange-rate and financial-account signs follow the definitions shown on the slides.

The IMF capital-flows paper is a conceptual reference. Its prose, figures and photographs are not reproduced.
