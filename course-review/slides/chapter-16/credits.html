Historical wage/unemployment illustration, 1861–1913: Bank of England, Thomas and Dimsdale (2017), A millennium of macroeconomic data for the UK, version 3.1, A47 column B (composite weekly earnings) and A50 column J (composite unemployment). Underlying wage sources: Crafts and Mills (1994) and Feinstein (1990); unemployment: Boyer and Hatton (2002) and Bank of England calculations. Data retrieved November 12, 2026. All 53 annual observations are included. Nominal weekly-earnings growth is 100 × (Wₜ/Wₜ₋₁ − 1), using full workbook precision; a separate OLS fit with intercept and Pearson correlation describes the association. Earnings cover England/Great Britain; unemployment uses historical UK boundaries. This is an original historical reconstruction using different measures from Phillips, not his original observations or fitted nonlinear curve. Phillips (1958) studied UK nominal hourly-wage growth and trade-union unemployment over 1861–1957, fitting the initial relationship over 1861–1913. Election studies

Original charts of selected numerical results: Fabio Alvim Klein (2010), Reelection incentives and political budget cycle: evidence from Brazil, Table 1. The source article is CC BY-NC 4.0; the chart is a new design of four published means, with election dates from TSE. This free course use is noncommercial. No licence is applied to the whole deck.

Shawn Cole (2008), HBS Working Paper 09-001, Table 3B: public-bank agricultural-credit coefficients, transformed to an index of 100 in the election-year reference; approximate 95% intervals use the reported standard errors. The published article appeared in 2009, but the plotted quantitative version is the 2008 working paper. Stein and Streb (2004), Table A.1: all 19 country-fixed-effects presidential-election month coefficients on log(1 + monthly depreciation), multiplied by 100, relative to months outside that window. Both charts show pooled study estimates, not one observed calendar series. Only selected numerical facts and original chart designs are included; no paper, copied graph or prose is redistributed.
