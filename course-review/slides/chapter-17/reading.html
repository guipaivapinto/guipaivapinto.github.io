Classical Macroeconomics

  • Before the 1930s, classical macroeconomics emphasized flexible wages and prices.
  • Self-correction: in the long run, wage and price adjustments return real GDP to potential output.
  • Classical model of the price level: with real money demand fixed, 10% more money means a 10% higher price level.
  • Limited stabilization: government intervention could delay adjustment or create inflation.

The Great Depression and Keynes

  • The Great Depression (1929–1939) challenged confidence in rapid self-correction.
  • In his 1936 book, The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money, John Maynard Keynes argued that weak aggregate demand could sustain unemployment.
  • Government policy could support aggregate demand and employment; fiscal policy became a central tool.

The Goal of Stabilization Policy

  • Fiscal and monetary policy aim to reduce output gaps and cyclical unemployment.
  • After a credit crisis, supporting spending can limit lost income and job losses.
  • Policy can smooth fluctuations; it cannot eliminate the business cycle.
