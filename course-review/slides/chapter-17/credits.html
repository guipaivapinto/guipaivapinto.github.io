Chapter 17: Sources

Macroeconomics: Events and Ideas · ECON_OX 112

Explanations, numerical examples and diagrams were independently written and drawn for this course. The stabilization diagram uses fictional GDP paths around unchanged potential output. The classical and Keynesian panels are original schematic AD–AS diagrams with no empirical scale; no textbook figure is reproduced.

The Great Depression chart uses U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis annual real GDP (A191RX / FRED GDPCA), billions of chained 2017 dollars, not seasonally adjusted, 1929–1941; retrieved November 19, 2026 from the FRED numerical table (series update September 30, 2026). Original transformation: divide each annual observation by its 1929 value and multiply by 100. Table changes compare annual average levels; shading marks broad annual phases, not exact monthly recessions or causal policy effects. BEA public-domain information policy. No agency logo or source chart is included.

The fiscal-history figure uses nineteen numerical observations for 1930–1948: OMB, FY2021 Historical Tables, Table 1.2 (published total federal budget balance as a percentage of fiscal-year GDP), and BLS/CEA, Economic Report of the President 2024, Table B–22 (calendar-year civilian unemployment rate). Retrieved November 19, 2026 through the official numerical tables. Original transformation: deficit = negative of the published OMB balance, so surpluses plot below zero. Do not subtract the separately rounded receipts/outlays columns. Original aligned panels and three cumulative data reveals; no textbook figure is reproduced. Fiscal and calendar years differ. Historical labor estimates and changes in age coverage limit comparability; wartime unemployment excludes armed forces. The figure illustrates chronology, not a fiscal multiplier estimate. OMB/CEA official statistical observations are U.S. federal-government material; BLS public-domain information and citation policy. No agency emblem is included.

The Greece exercise uses five official numerical observations per series from Bank of Greece, The Chronicle of the Great Crisis (2014), Table 9, printed page 85: October 2013 Eurostat/ELSTAT vintage. Debt and GDP are nominal millions of euros, converted to billions for the left panel; the right panel uses reported debt/GDP percentages. This is an original drawing, not a reproduced source chart. The 2012 debt reduction reflects restructuring; the figure does not isolate a causal fiscal effect. The Bank publication is not assumed to be public domain; only numerical facts are used.
