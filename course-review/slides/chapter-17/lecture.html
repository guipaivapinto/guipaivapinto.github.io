Classical Macroeconomics

  • Economists already studied business cycles, but disagreed about short-run adjustment and policy.
  • Self-correction: in the long run, wage and price adjustments return real GDP to potential output.
  • Classical model of the price level: with real money demand fixed, 10% more money means a 10% higher price level.
  • Limited stabilization: government intervention could delay adjustment or create inflation.

    The Keynesian Revolution

    • Keynes (1936): The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money.
    • Short-run focus: weak aggregate demand can keep output and employment below their potential.
    • Course models: consumption, planned spending and the multiplier developed from Keynesian ideas and later economists.
    • Policy: government spending and taxes can support demand when private spending is too weak.

    Animal Spirits and Business Cycles

    • Classical monetary emphasis: changes in the money supply shift aggregate demand.
    • Keynesian emphasis: business confidence can shift AD even when the money supply is unchanged.
    • Animal spirits: swings in business optimism and pessimism affect expected profits and planned investment.
    • Lower confidence: lower planned investment shifts AD left; with sticky wages and prices, output and employment fall.
    • Earlier in this course: our business-cycle model included both monetary and confidence shocks.
    RBA, Corporate Sentiment and Investment (2021) · Richmond Fed, Quantity Theory: Historical Evolution (1974)

    Activity: A Confidence Shock

    • Businesses cut planned investment. Money supply is unchanged, output is below potential, and wages and prices adjust slowly. Which change could offset the fall in aggregate demand?
    • A. Lower government purchases.
    • B. Higher government purchases.
    • C. Higher income taxes.
    • D. A lower money supply.

    B. Higher government purchases shift AD right. With idle resources, this can support output and employment.

    Policies to Fight Recession

    • Macroeconomic policy activism: using fiscal and monetary policy to reduce business-cycle fluctuations.
    • Keynes: public spending can restore jobs when private demand is weak.
    • Hayek: credit booms can misdirect investment; further stimulus can delay adjustment.
    • The 1930s: several governments used relief and public works without following one Keynesian program.
    • Roosevelt: New Deal support, retrenchment in 1937, then renewed deficit spending in 1938.
    Keynes–Hayek letters, October 1932 · FDR Library, Depression and New Deal

          Monetarism and Monetary Policy Rules

          • Monetarism: money supply growth plays a central role in nominal spending and inflation.
          • Friedman’s rule: increase the money supply at a steady, predictable rate.
          • Discretionary monetary policy: policy choices made case by case as economic conditions change, rather than following a fixed rule.
          • Why prefer a rule? Uncertain policy lags can make attempts to fine-tune the economy destabilizing.
          Federal Reserve Board, Jefferson (2024)

          Inflation and the Natural Rate of Unemployment

          Unemployment relative to NAIRUInflation pressure, other conditions fixed
          Below NAIRUInflation tends to rise
          At NAIRUInflation is stable
          Above NAIRUInflation tends to fall
          • NAIRU: Non-Accelerating Inflation Rate of Unemployment: the natural rate in this model, consistent with stable inflation, other conditions fixed.
          • Natural-rate hypothesis: expansionary policy cannot permanently keep unemployment below the natural rate.
          • Repeated expansion: holding unemployment below NAIRU requires accelerating inflation as expectations adjust.
          Federal Reserve Board, Powell (2018)

          Rational Expectations and New Classical Economics

          • Rational expectations: people use available information, including expected policy, when forecasting the future.
          • Learning: people can make mistakes, but policy cannot rely on the same predictable mistakes continuing.
          • New classical result: with flexible wages and prices, anticipated monetary expansion raises prices rather than real output.

          Example: if workers expect higher inflation, they seek higher nominal wages before the policy takes effect.

          Minneapolis Fed, Rational Expectations (1978)

          The Great Moderation, 1985–2007

          • 1985–2007: smaller fluctuations in GDP growth and lower, more stable inflation than in the 1970s and early 1980s.
          • Long expansions and periods of low unemployment encouraged confidence in economic stability.
          • The period still included recessions and financial shocks.

          Monetary Limits and Fiscal Policy

          • Near-zero policy rates left little room for further conventional rate cuts.
          • Through purchases of longer-term securities, quantitative easing (QE) expanded the monetary base and helped lower borrowing costs and support demand. Weak recovery renewed support for expansionary fiscal policy.
          • 2010–2013 fiscal consolidation: several European governments cut spending and raised taxes to reduce deficits.
          • Debt trade-off: budget deficits add to debt. Low interest costs ease debt service; weak GDP growth can raise debt/GDP.
          Federal Reserve · Unconventional policy (2015) · Federal Reserve · Monetary policy tools · ECB WP2040 · Fiscal consolidation, 2010–13

              Policy in a Low-Interest-Rate World

              • Secular stagnation: the natural real interest rate needed for full employment remains persistently below zero.
              • At the lower limit on nominal rates, low expected inflation can leave real rates too high for full employment.
              • Possible drivers include slower productivity growth, slower population growth, and aging that changes saving and investment.
              • The United States and euro area faced weak demand at low rates. Germany and Spain shared ECB policy, but borrowing costs differed.
