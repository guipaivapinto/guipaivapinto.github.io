Shifts in Money Demand

•Price level ↑ → the same real purchases require more dollars → MD shifts right.

•Real GDP ↑ → more real transactions at given prices → MD shifts right.

•Easier access to short-term credit can reduce the balances needed between paydays → MD may shift left.

•Faster, reliable banking and payments can reduce idle balances → MD may shift left.

•Institutions that require larger cash buffers can raise desired balances → MD shifts right.

The Federal Funds Rate

•Some banks lend reserve balances to other institutions overnight. The federal funds rate is the rate on those loans.

•The FOMC announces a target range; the market determines the effective rate.

•The Fed influences this overnight rate, which helps shape other short-term rates and broader financial conditions.

From a Fed Purchase to Money Supply

1Recall the previous class: the Fed buys a Treasury security from a nonbank seller.

2The payment credits the seller’s bank with reserves; the bank credits the seller’s deposit.

3The monetary base rises; the seller now holds a deposit instead of a security. In this model, MS shifts right.

4The interest rate adjusts along the existing MD curve. Policy did not directly shift MD.

Today’s ample-reserves system relies mainly on administered rates such as IORB to steer overnight rates. A rate cut does not require a matching bond purchase.

Why Maturity Matters

•A longer-term yield reflects the expected path of short-term rates and compensation for holding longer-term risk.

•A change in today’s policy rate may already be anticipated; a 10-year yield need not change by the same amount.

•Longer maturity is different from the macroeconomic long run. A two-year investment is longer than a one-year investment; the data chart uses a 10-year benchmark.

From Interest Rates to Aggregate Demand

1A lower policy rate can ease broader borrowing and financial conditions.

2Lower relevant real borrowing costs can encourage consumption and investment.

3A $10,000 project paying $10,500 next year loses money with a $10,600 repayment but gains with a $10,400 repayment.

4At a given price level, higher C and I raise planned expenditure: AD shifts right.

Transmission is conditional: expected inflation, credit access, risk, and confidence also matter.
