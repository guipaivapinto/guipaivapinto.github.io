Inflation and the Output Gap

Inflation target

A goal for inflation over time.

Output gap

Real GDP relative to potential GDP.

g = 100 × (Y − Y*) / Y*

Y: real GDP · Y*: potential GDP · g: output gap (%)

Y = 980; Y* = 1,000 → g = −2%

Fed inflation goal: 2% PCE.

The Taylor Rule

i = r* + π + 0.5(π − π*) + 0.5g

r*: neutral real rateπ: inflationπ*: targetg: output gap
2%4%2%−2%

Inflation above target: Raises the suggested rate.

Output below potential: Lowers the suggested rate.

Quantitative Easing

Action

The central bank buys large amounts of longer-term bonds.

Bond market

Higher bond prices mean lower yields.

Spending

Lower borrowing costs can support C and I.

Monetary Expansion

One-time MS change; long-run expected inflation unchanged.

Start

MS = 600; i = 4%.

Short run

MS rises to 800; i falls to 2%.

Long run

Prices rise. MD shifts right; i returns to 4%.

New MS stays at 800; potential output is unchanged.
