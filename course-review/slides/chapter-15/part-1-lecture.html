The Opportunity Cost of Holding Money

Keep $600 for one yearEnding amountInterest earned
Money at 0%$600$0
Bond at 1%$606$6
Bond at 6%$636$36

Rate of return = (ending amount − starting amount) ÷ starting amount

•For the 6% bond: $36 ÷ $600 = 6% per year.

•Keeping $600 in money means giving up that interest.

•When the bond rate rises from 1% to 6%, interest forgone rises from $6 to $36.

Shifts in Money Demand

Price level

Higher prices require more dollars for the same purchases. MD shifts right.

Real GDP

More output means more purchases and payments. MD shifts right.

Credit and banking technology

Easier credit or faster payments can reduce cash buffers. MD may shift left.

Banking institutions

Rules that require larger account balances raise money demand. MD shifts right.

Money Demand Checkpoint

1. Interest rates rise; prices and spending stay the same. Movement along MD or a shift?

Move up along MD: the quantity of money demanded falls.

2. At the same interest rate, the same bills cost more dollars. Movement or shift?

MD shifts right: more money is needed for payments.

The Federal Funds Rate

River Bank todayAmount
Reserves available$2,000
Payments due tonight$2,500
Overnight loan needed$500

•River Bank can borrow the missing $500 overnight from another bank. The rate it pays is the federal funds rate (FFR).

•More reserves reduce banks’ need to borrow; fewer reserves increase it. When reserves are scarce, this affects the FFR.

•The FOMC sets a target range for the FFR.

A Fed Bond Purchase

AccountThe $1,000 purchase
Federal Reserve

Treasury bond +$1,000

Bank reserves +$1,000

River Bank

Treasury bond −$1,000

Reserves: $2,000

+ $1,000 → $3,000

Overnight loan needed: $500 → $0

The Fed buys a $1,000 Treasury bond from River Bank.

The Fed pays by adding $1,000 to the bank’s reserves: $2,000 becomes $3,000.

The bank can cover its $2,500 payment. It no longer needs the $500 overnight loan.

In the textbook model, more reserves can support new bank loans. New loans create deposits and raise the money supply.

The bond purchase adds reserves. New bank lending creates deposits.

Money-Market Checkpoint

1. Money supply rises while MD stays fixed. What happens to the interest rate, and why?

MS shifts right. People buy bonds with excess money, raising bond prices and lowering the rate.

2. The rate is below equilibrium. How does the money shortage disappear?

People sell bonds. Bond prices fall and the rate rises, reducing the quantity of money demanded.

Monetary Policy and Aggregate Demand

Total planned spending = C + I + G + NX

1The Fed lowers its policy rate. Borrowing rates for households and firms can fall.

2Cheaper car and appliance loans encourage household purchases: consumption (C) rises.

3Cheaper business loans make more equipment and building projects worthwhile: investment (I) rises.

4At the same price level, higher C and I raise planned spending. AD shifts right.

Higher borrowing rates reverse this chain: C and I fall, and AD shifts left.

Borrowing Costs and Investment

1A bakery considers a $10,000 oven. Expected net receipts next year are $10,500.

2Borrow at 6%: repay $10,600. The project loses $100.

3Borrow at 4%: repay $10,400. The project gains $100.
