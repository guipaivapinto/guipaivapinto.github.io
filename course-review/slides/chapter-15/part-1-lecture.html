Shifts in Money Demand

•Price level ↑ → the same real purchases require more dollars → MD shifts right.

•Real GDP ↑ → more real transactions at given prices → MD shifts right.

•Easier access to short-term credit can reduce the balances needed between paydays → MD may shift left.

•Faster, reliable banking and payments can reduce idle balances → MD may shift left.

•Institutions that require larger cash buffers can raise desired balances → MD shifts right.

Money Demand Checkpoint

1. The short-term interest rate rises while prices, real GDP and payment arrangements stay fixed. Movement along MD or a shift?

A movement upward along MD: the higher opportunity cost reduces the quantity of money demanded.

2. At the same interest rate, the same basket of bills costs more dollars. Movement or shift?

MD shifts right: people need larger nominal balances for the same real purchases.

The Federal Funds Rate

•Some banks lend reserve balances to other institutions overnight. The federal funds rate is the rate on those loans.

•The FOMC announces a target range; the market determines the effective rate.

•The Fed influences this overnight rate, which helps shape other short-term rates and broader financial conditions.

From a Fed Purchase to Money Supply

1Recall the previous class: the Fed buys a Treasury security from a nonbank seller.

2The payment credits the seller’s bank with reserves; the bank credits the seller’s deposit.

3The monetary base rises; the seller now holds a deposit instead of a security. In this model, MS shifts right.

4The interest rate adjusts along the existing MD curve. Policy did not directly shift MD.

Today’s ample-reserves system relies mainly on administered rates such as IORB to steer overnight rates. A rate cut does not require a matching bond purchase.

Expansionary Monetary Policy

MD = 1,000 − 100i. Fixed prices, real GDP and money’s own return.

Initial equilibrium

Start at MS₀ = 600 and i₀ = 4%.

Money supply increases

In the money-supply model, a purchase raises MS to 800.

Asset-market adjustment

At 4%, people hold more money than they desire; they seek bonds and other assets.

New equilibrium

Bond prices rise and yields fall. New equilibrium: i₁ = 2%, M₁ = 800.

Adjustment from a High Interest Rate

MD = 1,000 − 100i. Fixed prices, real GDP and money’s own return.

A rate above equilibrium

Suppose i = 6%: MD = 400 but MS = 600. Desired money holdings are below the available stock.

Excess money balances

People try to exchange excess balances for bonds. The money remains in the system when it changes hands.

Bond prices rise; yields fall

Bidding raises bond prices and lowers yields. A $100 one-year payoff rises from $94.34 to $96.15.

Money-market equilibrium

At i = 4%, desired and available money both equal 600: the adjustment pressure ends.

Money-Market Checkpoint

1. Money supply increases while money demand is unchanged. Which curve moves, and what happens to the equilibrium short-term rate?

MS moves right. At the old rate there are excess money balances; asset purchases raise bond prices and lower the equilibrium rate.

2. At a rate below equilibrium, desired money exceeds the available stock. What adjustment removes the shortage?

People sell bonds to obtain money. Bond prices fall, yields rise and the quantity of money demanded falls toward the fixed stock.

•Explain how monetary policy affects consumption, investment, and aggregate demand.

•Interpret policy-induced AD shifts while holding short-run aggregate supply fixed.

•Demonstrate expansionary and contractionary effects on output and the price level.

From Interest Rates to Aggregate Demand

1A lower policy rate can ease broader borrowing and financial conditions.

2Lower relevant real borrowing costs can encourage consumption and investment.

3A $10,000 project paying $10,500 next year loses money with a $10,600 repayment but gains with a $10,400 repayment.

4At a given price level, higher C and I raise planned expenditure: AD shifts right.

Transmission is conditional: expected inflation, credit access, risk, and confidence also matter.

The economy is below potential. Monetary easing lowers relevant real borrowing costs, and SRAS is fixed. Predict the direction of C, I, AD, output and the price level.

Original index model: AD shifts; SRAS and LRAS stay fixed.

A negative output gap

Begin below potential output: Y₀ = 98, while Y* = 100.

Financial conditions ease

Easier financial conditions encourage interest-sensitive C and I.

Aggregate demand moves right

AD shifts right, from AD₀ to AD₁; SRAS and LRAS stay fixed.

The short-run equilibrium

In this short-run model, output and the price level rise to 100.
